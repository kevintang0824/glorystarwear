Direct answer

What information belongs on a U.S. clothing label?

For most textile and wool products sold in the United States, the FTC requires labels that disclose fiber content, country of origin, and the identity of the manufacturer or another responsible business. A qualifying business name or Registered Identification Number may be used for identity; an RN is optional. A separate FTC rule requires manufacturers and importers of covered apparel to provide regular care instructions and any necessary warnings on a permanent, legible label supported by a reasonable basis.

Do not send a factory a copied label image and assume it is compliant. First identify the exact product, fibers, origin, responsible business, intended market and sales route; have a qualified owner decide which rules and exceptions apply; substantiate the care route for the finished garment; then release controlled copy, artwork, placement, SKU mapping, physical sample evidence, and approval. This article is a planning aid, not legal advice, compliance certification, or a substitute for current product-specific review.

Scope first

Confirm the product and rule route before applying a template

“Sportswear” is a commercial category, not one legal label category. Product construction, fiber, wearer, use, destination, and marketing claims can change the review.

  1. 1

    Identify the sellable product

    Record the style, components, fiber-bearing sections, trim and lining, size and color scope, set or separate-sale status, intended user, use, U.S. sales route, and every variant that may require different label copy.

  2. 2

    Name the responsible decision owner

    The brand, importer, manufacturer, distributor, or other responsible business should assign a qualified person to determine applicability, approve identity and origin, select evidence, and close exceptions. A factory can supply product facts and execute approved files but should not silently invent legal copy.

  3. 3

    Resolve specialty routes

    Check whether wool, fur, children’s products, protective claims, flammability requirements, state requirements, retailer policies, marketplace rules, or another product-specific regime adds or changes obligations. Do not treat this baseline textile checklist as a complete U.S. compliance file.

  4. 4

    Approve the consumer-facing and online statements together

    Align labels, hangtags, packaging, product pages, catalogs, marketplace fields, fiber or performance claims, origin claims, and care statements so one channel does not contradict the controlled product record.

Care evidence

Write care instructions from the finished garment, not a fabric guess

The FTC says covered businesses need a reasonable basis for care instructions and warnings. Evidence must support what happens when the complete product is repeatedly cared for as directed.

Washing or drycleaning

Release at least the required regular cleaning route and the relevant conditions. Do not state “Dryclean Only” without evidence that washing would harm the product.

Bleach, drying, and ironing

Review what consumers may reasonably assume when the label is silent. Add conditions or warnings where the normal procedure could cause substantial harm.

Multi-component garments

Consider fabric, thread, elastic, prints, transfers, embroidery, pads, zippers, trims, coatings, labels, and color combinations. Component results need a justified connection to the garment as a whole.

Change control

Reopen the care decision when a fabric, dye, color, finish, decoration, trim, construction, supplier, or production route changes the evidence basis.

Sources and method

Reddit identifies the uncertainty; FTC guidance controls the factual answer

A recent July 2026 small-business discussion asks what fiber, origin, care, and business-identity labels a small U.S. apparel brand needs. That thread is used as demand evidence only; Reddit comments do not establish the law, determine a product's origin, approve label placement, or prove that a care statement is supported.

The factual baseline comes from the FTC's current Textile and Wool labeling guide, Care Labeling Rule business guide, and RN database guidance. Those sources explain the baseline requirements and exceptions; a responsible business still needs current product-specific review before sale.

FAQ

U.S. private-label clothing label questions

What information is generally required on U.S. textile clothing labels?

For most covered textile and wool products, FTC guidance identifies fiber content, country of origin, and the identity of the manufacturer or another responsible business. Covered apparel also needs care instructions and necessary warnings under the Care Labeling Rule. Product-specific rules and exceptions still need review.

Does a private-label clothing brand need an RN?

No. The FTC says an RN is optional. A qualifying business can use its name, or it may use a valid RN in place of the name. Confirm the identity route and active RN status before approving artwork.

Can a factory write the care instructions for a brand?

A supplier can provide construction, material, process, test, and experience inputs, but the responsible business should assign a qualified owner to approve the final instructions and warnings. The released care statement needs a reasonable basis for the finished garment.

Can one care label be copied across every color and style?

Only when the responsible owner has evidence that the same product scope, materials, colors, components, decoration, construction, origin, care behavior, and other required information remain applicable. Any relevant change should reopen the label review.

Are these four label fields the complete U.S. compliance checklist?

No. Wool, fur, children's products, flammability, state rules, special claims, customer policies, marketplaces, and other product-specific requirements may add obligations. Use this article to structure the handoff, then obtain current qualified review for the actual product and sales route.
