Physical sample to production record

Reference Garment vs Tech Pack: What Should You Send a Manufacturer?

A physical garment can communicate shape and construction quickly. A controlled tech pack records what to reproduce, what to change, how to measure it, and which revision can move into production.

Download Handoff ChecklistBuild a Tech Pack

Direct answer

Can a reference garment replace a tech pack?

Usually not for repeatable custom production. A reference garment is useful evidence for silhouette, proportion, assembly, handfeel, or a feature you want to discuss, but it does not state which details are intentional, what must change, how measurements were taken, which materials are approved, how other sizes are graded, or who owns the resulting files. Use the garment to start development, then convert every keep, change, propose, and unknown decision into a revision-controlled specification before bulk release.

By GloryStarWear product development teamPublished September 23, 2026For clothing founders, product developers, and sourcing buyersFollow the RSS feed

Reference garment

Shows one physical configuration at one size and condition. It can make shape, balance, feature placement, and construction discussions more concrete.

Tech pack

Names the drawings, measurements, materials, construction, artwork, labels, packaging, tolerances, revisions, and approvals that control the product.

Development sample

Tests the factory's interpretation of the current brief. It reveals questions; it does not silently fill every missing specification.

Bulk reference

Connects the approved physical sample to the released tech pack, material codes, artwork, labels, packaging, exceptions, and decision owner.

Use the garment and the tech pack for different jobs

The strongest handoff combines physical evidence with explicit instructions. Mark what is controlled, what is only inspiration, and what the manufacturer must propose.

Eight-step handoff

Turn a physical reference into sample-ready instructions

Do not ask a supplier to guess which parts of the garment matter. Build a record that separates observation, instruction, proposal, and approval.

  1. Identify the reference

    Assign a reference ID, photograph front, back, inside, labels, and relevant details, record the labeled size and condition, and state whether the garment may be marked or opened.

  2. Confirm rights and allowed use

    Record the owner, source, permission, intended use, excluded logos or artwork, confidentiality needs, return instructions, and any design-right review. Do not assume possession grants copying rights.

  3. Mark keep, change, propose, and unknown

    For every visible feature, state whether it should be retained, modified to a defined target, proposed by the supplier, or left open for a named decision.

  4. Measure with a defined method

    Record the garment state, preparation, landmarks, tools, orientation, and point-of-measure diagrams. Body measurements and garment measurements are different records; use consistent definitions.

  5. Replace appearance guesses with material records

    Use coded swatches and current supplier data for fabric, lining, elastic, trim, thread, and color. A reference garment may not reveal fiber content, finish, prior care, or supply identity.

  6. Document construction and artwork

    Reconcile technical views with seam purpose, stitch and edge direction, reinforcement, closures, pockets, lining, labels, and rights-cleared production artwork.

  7. Sample the interpretation

    Give the development sample a version, purpose, required evidence, acceptance questions, approver, due decision, and open issues. Compare it against both the physical reference and the written change list.

  8. Release one production record

    After fit, materials, construction, artwork, labels, packaging, sizes, and exceptions are resolved, link the approved sample ID to the current tech pack and preserve later changes as new revisions.

Important limits

What one garment cannot prove

A useful reference reduces ambiguity, but it remains one item with an incomplete history.

Original specification

Measurement, fiber, finish, color, stitch, tolerance, and care history may differ from the product's original released record.

All sizes and colors

One garment cannot prove grade rules, fit transitions, opacity, shade, construction stability, or performance across a full range.

Supply continuity

A similar-looking fabric or trim is not proof of the same mill, lot, process, availability, minimum, price, or future reorder result.

Intellectual-property clearance

A physical item does not show which design, trademark, artwork, pattern, or technical assets may be reused in each market.

Current primary references

Use standards and qualified review for the decisions they actually cover

ISO 8559-1:2017, confirmed current in 2026, provides anthropometric definitions for body measurement; it does not replace garment measurement diagrams or fit approval. ASTM D6193-16(2025) classifies stitch and seam types and describes their general characteristics; the project must still select the applicable construction. WIPO's fashion and IP guidance explains that design rights, trademarks, copyright, and patents can affect fashion assets. Rights depend on the asset and jurisdiction, so obtain qualified advice where copying or ownership is uncertain.

FAQ

Reference garment and tech pack questions

Can a clothing manufacturer copy a sample garment exactly?

A factory can inspect and develop from a permitted reference, but exact copying is not a usable acceptance rule. Identify what may be reproduced, define every intended change, replace unknown materials with approved codes, document measurements and construction, clear relevant rights, and approve the factory sample against the released record.

Do I need a tech pack if I already have a physical sample?

You can begin discussion or development with a physical sample, but repeatable custom production still needs a controlled specification. The approved record should state materials, measurements, construction, artwork, labels, packaging, quality criteria, revisions, exceptions, and the relationship to the physical sample.

Can a tech pack replace a physical sample?

No document proves the finished garment by itself. A tech pack controls instructions; physical samples test interpretation, fit, material behavior, construction, appearance, and packaging. Bulk release should connect the approved sample to the matching file revisions.

Should the manufacturer cut open the reference garment?

Only with explicit written permission. Define whether non-destructive review is enough, what internal construction must be inspected, who owns the garment, how destructive review will be documented, and whether the item must be returned.

Who owns the new pattern and tech pack?

Do not infer ownership from who paid for sampling or supplied the reference. State ownership, permitted use, editable source-file delivery, confidentiality, reuse restrictions, return or retention, and termination treatment in the written agreement; obtain qualified advice for the relevant jurisdiction when needed.

Starting from a physical sample?

Send the reference, keep-change list, target sizes, quantity plan, and known material direction.

Unknowns can remain open when they have an owner and decision gate. The first useful outcome is a sample-ready brief, not an unsupported promise to copy everything.
