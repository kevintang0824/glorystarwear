First Activewear Collection Manufacturing Checklist

Turn a launch idea into a focused range, controlled supplier brief, purposeful sample plan, and repeatable production record.

Direct answer

How do you plan a first activewear collection for manufacturing?

Start with one defined customer and use case, then build the smallest coherent range that can test your product idea. Choose a development route for each style, state quantity and commercial boundaries, control fabric, fit, construction, branding and packaging, assign a purpose to every sample, define bulk-release evidence, and preserve the approved record for reorders. A supplier can confirm MOQ, price, sample cost and timing only after reviewing that specific scope.

By GloryStarWear product development teamPublished and updated August 29, 2026For startup activewear brands and first collection buyersFollow the RSS feed

Focus the customer

Name the activity, wearer, market, channel, price position, climate, movement, coverage, and problem the range is intended to solve.

Reduce uncontrolled variety

Give each style, color, size and material a reason to exist. Added variety can create separate development, minimum, testing, packing, and inventory exposure.

Build evidence gates

Connect the specification to coded swatches, production artwork, measured samples, wear checks, approved labels, packed samples, and inspection records.

Plan the second order

Keep approved references, version history, supplier assumptions, packaging data, and size-level results so a reorder is controlled rather than reconstructed.

Give every first-collection SKU a commercial job

A larger assortment is not automatically a stronger launch. Map each product to a customer need, selling role, development risk, shared material strategy, and next-order decision.

Development route

Choose stock, light-custom, ODM, OEM, or a hybrid by style

Route names vary between suppliers. Define responsibilities and deliverables instead of assuming the label explains what is included.

Stock or white label

Useful when a verified existing product, fit, fabric and available color route meet the brief. Confirm product identity, inventory status, branding limits, packing, and replenishment.

Light customization

Start from an existing base and control selected colors, logos, labels, trims, or packaging. Record which base specifications cannot change and which inputs create new minimums.

ODM development

Ask the supplier to develop from a commercial or design brief. Define ownership, source files, decision responsibility, sample outputs, revisions, and how the approved result becomes controlled.

OEM production

Provide production-ready drawings, materials, measurements, construction, artwork, labels, packaging, quality criteria, and revisions. Factory review and physical sampling still remain necessary.

Route comparison

Use responsibility, not vocabulary, to compare suppliers

For a fuller responsibility map, use the OEM vs ODM sportswear guide. For small first orders, separate the style, material, color, decoration, label and packaging constraints in the activewear MOQ guide.

Fourteen decisions

Move from idea to a quoteable, sample-ready collection

These stages are not a fixed calendar. Their dependencies show what must be decided, verified, or left visibly open before the next commitment.

  1. Write the commercial brief

    Define customer, use, market, channel, price position, launch goal, delivery country, and the product problem being solved.

  2. Map the smallest coherent range

    Name the hero style and the function of every supporting style. Remove products that add variables without testing a meaningful customer or commercial assumption.

  3. Assign a development route

    For each style, state whether the project uses a stock base, light customization, ODM support, OEM specifications, or a controlled hybrid.

  4. Build the quantity architecture

    Plan units by style, color, size and delivery wave. Keep forecast assumptions separate from supplier-confirmed minimums and component commitments.

  5. Define the commercial boundary

    Record target product cost, currency, delivery term, destination, packaging, testing, duties, freight and other landed-cost components that must be compared.

  6. Specify fabrics and trims

    Connect every material to a code, construction, composition, finished weight, color, stretch direction, recovery, finish, performance need, evidence and approved swatch.

  7. Control fit and sizing

    Define wearer, base size, points of measure, methods, tolerances, grade logic, fit sample, representative sizes, movement and wash checks.

  8. Document construction

    Reconcile technical views, panels, seams, stitches, allowances, reinforcements, edges, pockets, closures, linings and assembly access.

  9. Release production artwork

    Provide rights-cleared source files, method, substrate, final dimensions, coordinates, color references, orientation, filename and physical approval route.

  10. Hand off labels and packaging

    Separate stable brand data from variable SKU, size, barcode and destination data. Define placement, folding, individual packs, inserts, cartons and packed-sample approval.

  11. Give every sample a purpose

    Name the decisions, evidence, approver and exit rule for development, fit, size-set, pre-production, decoration, packaging or shipment-reference samples.

  12. Define quality and claim evidence

    Connect material, fit, construction, decoration, labels, packing and any performance claim to applicable checks, records, scope and release authority.

  13. Back-plan the launch from dependencies

    Allow for buyer decisions, material availability, sample review, revisions, testing, artwork, labels, packing, production, inspection and transport without treating an estimate as a guarantee.

  14. Prepare reorder control before launch

    Retain the approved specification, physical reference, reports, component data, packaging version, supplier assumptions and order results; record every later substitution or change.

FAQ

First activewear collection manufacturing questions

What information should I prepare before contacting an activewear manufacturer?

Prepare the target customer and use, product references or technical files, styles, colors, size range, quantity assumptions, target market, fabric and performance priorities, artwork, labels, packaging, delivery country, timing need, budget boundary, and open questions. Mark estimates clearly so the supplier can return assumptions.

How many styles should a first activewear collection include?

There is no universal number. A smaller coherent range usually reduces simultaneous fit, fabric, color, artwork, minimum, sample, packaging, inventory, and launch variables. Keep a style when it serves a defined customer or commercial role and the project can fund and verify its full commitment.

Should a startup choose OEM, ODM, or private label activewear?

Choose by the inputs you control and the development support you need. Existing or light-custom bases can reduce new decisions, ODM assigns more development work to the supplier, and OEM follows a buyer-controlled specification. A hybrid route can assign a different model to each style, but responsibilities and deliverables must be written.

Do I need a tech pack for my first activewear collection?

A full tech pack is strongest when the buyer controls the design and expects repeatable custom production. An early ODM or light-custom project may begin with a structured brief, but the approved product still needs a controlled record covering materials, measurements, construction, artwork, labels, packaging, quality criteria, revisions, and physical references before bulk.

When can a manufacturer confirm MOQ, price, samples, and lead time?

A supplier can give a reliable project-specific answer after reviewing the actual styles, quantities, colors, size split, materials, construction, decoration, labels, packaging, quality or testing scope, delivery term, destination, files, approval route, and current availability. Treat earlier website figures or ranges as planning context, not a fixed quotation.

Planning a first collection?

Send the range, quantity plan, references, and decisions you already have.

Unknowns can remain open. A structured brief helps separate feasible routes, required evidence, supplier assumptions, and the next sample decision.
