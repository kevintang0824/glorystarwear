Direct answer

What should a new sportswear product development brief include?

A useful development brief should define the target customer and market, launch or reorder goal, collection role, planned styles, development route, product references, intended use, fabric and fit direction, colorways, size range, quantity plan, artwork, labels, packaging, required claim evidence, sample approvals, decision owners, and target launch date.

Development decisions

Give every planned style a role, reference, and approval path

A collection becomes quoteable when product ideas are separated into style-level records and the buyer can see which decisions are fixed, proposed, under review, or approved.

Decision stageRecord before developmentControlled output
Commercial roleTarget customer, market, use case, launch or reorder goal, price position, sales channel, and the purpose of each style.A focused assortment in which every proposed style has a reason to remain, change, combine, or be removed.
Development routeWhether the style starts from an existing silhouette, a supplier-developed reference, or buyer-controlled drawings and specifications.A light-custom, ODM, or OEM route with clear responsibility for pattern, material, fit, artwork, and technical files.
Style specificationStyle code, reference, intended use, fit, size range, colorways, fabric direction, construction priorities, decoration, labels, and packaging.A style record ready to expand into measurements, bill of materials, artwork, sample comments, and costing assumptions.
Assortment and quantityCore, seasonal, entry, premium, team, accessory, or packaging role plus planned units by style, color, and size.A SKU plan that exposes complexity, dependencies, component minimums, and decisions that need supplier confirmation.
Sample approvalSample stage, revision, fabric and color references, fit result, artwork, labels, packaging, open issues, approval owner, and decision date.One traceable reference for the next sample or bulk release instead of approvals spread across messages and files.
Launch handoffRequired evidence, care and claim review, SKU rules, barcode data, packing, carton allocation, content assets, and delivery dependency.A launch-ready handoff that connects the approved product to inspection, packing, ecommerce, retail, team, or distributor needs.

Buyer questions

New sportswear product development FAQs

Can I request product development without a complete tech pack?

Yes. Start with a development brief containing the target customer, market, product list, references, intended use, size range, quantity plan, fabric and fit direction, artwork, packaging, delivery country, and decisions the supplier should propose.

What is the difference between light custom, ODM, and OEM development?

Light custom normally starts from an existing silhouette and changes brand details. ODM adds supplier development input for fit, material, trims, or product architecture. OEM follows buyer-controlled specifications such as patterns, measurements, bills of materials, artwork, and technical files.

How many styles should a first sportswear collection include?

There is no universal number. Choose a focused range that fits the target customer, budget, quantity plan, material and color dependencies, sampling capacity, launch timing, and ability to approve every SKU before bulk production.

What should be approved before bulk production?

Approve the product and sample revision, material and color references, measurements and fit, construction, artwork, labels, care information, packaging, size and quantity breakdown, required evidence, open exceptions, and responsible release owner.
