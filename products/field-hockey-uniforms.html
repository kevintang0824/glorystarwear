Sport-specific decision map

Resolve field-hockey posture, skort coverage, goalkeeper distinction, and club allocation

Field hockey has a different movement and garment system from rugby and ice hockey. The product brief should be built around low stick posture, the chosen women or youth block, field-player bottoms, and clearly separated goalkeeper needs.

01

Low stick posture and armhole movement

Review the top while sprinting, lunging, rotating, reaching across the body, and holding a low stick position. Confirm shoulder and side-panel mobility, armhole and neckline coverage, bust and hip balance, top length, hem movement, base-layer interaction, and number placement across the intended women's, youth, or unisex fit block.

02

Skirt, skort, short, and liner system

Choose the field-player bottom by club and competition context. Define rise, waistband, skirt overlap, skort liner length, short inseam, gusset, leg opening, pocket policy, opacity, stretch, seam profile, and coverage through running and low posture. Approve liner handfeel and outer-to-inner color relationship on the actual sample.

03

Goalkeeper contrast and sock coordination

Separate goalkeeper tops and layers from field-player colorways, fits, protection interfaces, numbers, and packing groups. Coordinate sock size, length, color, structure, and club artwork with each kit, but confirm garment compatibility with any protective equipment through the buyer's identified sample review rather than assuming one universal goalkeeper setup.

04

School, club, and season roster control

Structure the roster by field player or goalkeeper, top, skirt, skort or short, sock, warm-up layer, women's, youth, or unisex fit block, colorway, player size, and number. Add spares and late-player rules, then retain the approved measurements, artwork, color references, packing list, carton marks, and reorder codes for the next season.

Direct buyer answers

Field Hockey Uniforms sourcing questions, answered clearly

These short answers define what can be reviewed now, what the buyer should provide, and which terms still require project confirmation.

Can the field hockey uniforms project be quoted from a reference image alone?

No. A reference image can identify a field hockey uniforms direction, but a useful review also needs the requested garments, intended use, buyer market, quantity and size split, material and branding goals, packing, delivery country, and target date. Feasibility is confirmed only after the submitted project brief is reviewed.

How are fit, material, and construction routes approved?

Convert the intended movement and fit into measurable requirements, approve coded material swatches, and review the proposed construction on an identified project sample. The suggestions on this page are planning inputs, not approved specifications or universal performance claims.

How are MOQ, samples, and lead time confirmed?

MOQ, sample cost, sample rounds, and lead time are confirmed after reviewing quantity by player role, top and bottom option, women's, youth, or unisex fit block, colorway, player size, number, spare allocation, team pack, country, and season date. No universal figure on this page is a quotation or production commitment.

What evidence should be checked before bulk release?

Bulk-release evidence: Approve the identified field-player and goalkeeper samples, low-posture movement, coverage, measurements, artwork, liner and seam comfort, roster, packing plan, and reorder record. Branding handoff: Control club crest, sponsor marks, player numbers, goalkeeper contrast, home and away colorways, embroidery or prints, labels, and player, team, or retail packing.
