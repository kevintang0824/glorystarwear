Product directions

Define the exact custom motocross jerseys range before sampling

Use this page for upper-body motocross apparel, not riding pants, protective equipment, certified PPE, or weather outerwear. A jersey does not replace or certify a helmet, goggles, chest or back protector, neck brace, airbag, shoulder, elbow or knee protection, gloves, boots, or body armor.

Range and silhouette planning

  • Long-sleeve race jerseys, practice jerseys, youth and adult jersey blocks, alternate race colorways, pit-crew shirts, mechanic polos, off-bike team tees, supporter jerseys, and retail versions.
  • Confirm neckline, shoulder, armhole, sleeve and cuff, torso width and length, drop-tail direction, youth or adult block, and ease over buyer-named protective equipment; fit review does not certify equipment compatibility, protection, or competition acceptance.
  • Buyer and use context: motocross teams, clubs, training schools, race promoters, motorsport brands, retailers, distributors, dealers, and private-label apparel programs.

Material and construction review

  • State target composition, weight, airflow, stretch, handfeel, surface, opacity, color, snag or abrasion expectation, and decoration route on coded swatches; cooling, wicking, tear, abrasion, or durability claims require an agreed test and result.
  • Review neckline, shoulder and underarm panels, sleeve shape, cuff, side seams, hems, labels, decoration edges, and wear points against the supplied fit reference. Reinforcement or heavier material remains apparel construction and must not be described as impact or protective performance.
  • Define standing and seated riding posture, forward reach, elbow flexion, torso rotation, transitions, heat, dust, base-layer use, protective-equipment placement, practice or race duration, and wash conditions without treating an apparel sample as safety evidence.

Branding, order, and approval control

  • Control rider family name, race number, team identity, sponsor hierarchy, front, back and sleeve graphics, number contrast and clear space, visibility when a back protector is worn, labels, and rider, team, pit, or retail packing.
  • Quantity by race, practice, pit, mechanic, supporter, or retail jersey, youth or adult block, size, colorway, rider name, race number, sponsor version, spare allocation, packing group, destination, and event date.
  • Approve an identified jersey sample, measurements, riding-posture observations, fit notes over the buyer-named equipment reference, neckline and cuff review, material and artwork version, number visibility, organizer instructions, rider allocation, packing, and reorder record.

Sport-specific decision map

Separate motocross apparel from PPE while controlling gear fit, riding posture, numbers, and team versions

A motocross jersey is an apparel layer, not body armor or outerwear. Record the protective-equipment reference, current event rules, rider posture, name and number layout, sponsor version, allocation, and evidence without implying safety certification.

01

Apparel versus protective equipment

State clearly that the product is a jersey only. It does not replace or certify a helmet, goggles, chest or back protector, neck brace, airbag, shoulder, elbow or knee protection, gloves, boots, or body armor. The rider, team, equipment supplier, scrutineer, and organizer retain responsibility for current protective-equipment rules, compatibility, inspection, and use.

02

Fit over equipment and riding posture

Review neckline, shoulder, armhole, sleeve, cuff, torso ease, body length, hem, and graphics while standing and seated on the bike, reaching forward, bending the elbows, rotating, and wearing the exact buyer-named equipment reference. Record restriction, riding, bunching, snagging, visibility, or pressure without converting fit observations into a safety claim.

03

Rider name, number, sponsor, and protector visibility

Map the rider family name, allocated race number, contrast, outline, clear space, team identity, sponsor hierarchy, and front, back, shoulder, and sleeve graphics. Confirm the current event rules and whether a back protector covers the jersey, since required identity may need to remain visible on the outermost approved layer.

04

Race, practice, pit, and retail allocation

Separate race jerseys, practice jerseys, alternates, pit-crew shirts, mechanic polos, supporter pieces, retail units, and replacements. Preserve garment code, fit block, size, rider, name, number, sponsor version, labels, individual or team pack, carton, destination, and reorder record for each release.
