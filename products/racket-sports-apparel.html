Six planning gates

Turn the collection idea into a controlled club or retail program

  1. Name the customer and playing context

    Record sport, skill or club group, age route where relevant, indoor or outdoor venue, season, climate, frequency of use, and whether the item is a uniform, retail product, event garment, or social layer.

  2. Assign one role to every product

    Separate match, training, warm-up, travel, staff, participant, retail, fan, and accessory items. Remove duplicate styles that do not close a clear customer or program need.

  3. Define the collection system

    Control color families, artwork hierarchy, fabric direction, fit routes, size range, labels, trims, packaging, and the garment-specific decisions that must remain consistent.

  4. Forecast sizes and allocation

    Use roster, registration, retail, preorder, or prior-sales evidence. Keep top and bottom demand independent where customers may need different sizes, and record the basis for any buying curve.

  5. Choose the approval matrix

    Name which garments, colors, sizes, ball-pocket routes, liners, artwork methods, labels, and packed configurations require physical samples or evidence before release.

  6. Plan delivery and reorders

    Set the required event or launch date, allocation destination, pack hierarchy, carton marks, late changes, replacement process, repeat style codes, approved files, and owner of the next order.

FAQ

Racket-sports collection planning questions

What belongs in a racket-sports apparel collection?

Choose products by the actual program: match or court tops, shorts, skorts, dresses, warm-ups, social layers, coach or staff apparel, socks, visors, caps, wristbands, towels, and bags. Each item should have a named customer, use, quantity, size route, artwork, packaging, and reorder role.

Should tennis, pickleball, padel, and badminton use the same apparel range?

They can share fabrics, colors, trims, and some silhouettes, but the buyer should record sport-specific player behavior, ball-storage expectations, venue, climate, fit, social or retail use, and price architecture before treating one assortment as universal.

How should a club plan apparel quantities?

Start with a controlled roster, registration export, preorder, retail history, or documented size-demand assumption. Separate player, staff, event, retail, replacement, and sample quantities and keep top and bottom sizes independent when the program allows mixed sizing.

What should be approved before a racket-apparel reorder?

Confirm the active style and pattern revisions, fabric and color references, artwork files, size data, labels, packaging, packed reference, previous issues, supplier availability, quantities, destinations, and required date. Do not assume an old visual reference remains production-ready.
