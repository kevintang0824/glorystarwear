Sport-specific decision map

Control tri-suit format, three-discipline fit, transition construction, and athlete approval

A triathlon suit should not inherit a cycling skinsuit, running kit, or generic endurance specification unchanged. Name the race format and review how one garment behaves through swim, bike, run, and transition.

01

One-piece, two-piece, and race format

Name the event, distance, athlete level, sleeved or sleeveless direction, one-piece or two-piece route, wet or dry conditions, wetsuit interaction, carried nutrition, number attachment, and current organizer requirements. Keep short-course, long-course, training, and retail assumptions separate until the project brief confirms them.

02

Swim, bike, run, and transition fit

Review torso, shoulder, armhole, neckline, waist, hip, rise, inseam, leg opening, sleeve, compression, and coverage through swim reach, cycling posture, running stride, bending, and transition changes. Record restriction, gaping, riding, pressure, wet opacity, recovery, water retention observations, and chafe points by sample size.

03

Zipper, chamois, grippers, pockets, and seams

Map zipper route and guard, chamois or padding direction, panel joins, gusset, low-profile seams, sleeve and leg grippers, pocket position and load, labels, hems, and number attachment. Review these features across all three disciplines; a cycling-only or standing fit check is not sufficient approval evidence.

04

Athlete artwork, event checks, and allocation

Map club, team, athlete or country identity, sponsors, event marks, number zone, front, back, side, sleeve and leg artwork, color contrast, and size-dependent scaling. Link the dated artwork to the athlete, fit block, size, suit version, event instructions, individual pack, spare allocation, and reorder record.

Direct buyer answers

Custom Triathlon Suits sourcing questions, answered clearly

These short answers define what can be reviewed now, what the buyer should provide, and which terms still require project confirmation.

Can the custom triathlon suits project be quoted from a reference image alone?

No. A reference image can identify a custom triathlon suits direction, but a useful review also needs the requested garments, intended use, buyer market, quantity and size split, material and branding goals, packing, delivery country, and target date. Feasibility is confirmed only after the submitted project brief is reviewed.

How are fit, material, and construction routes approved?

Convert the intended movement and fit into measurable requirements, approve coded material swatches, and review the proposed construction on an identified project sample. The suggestions on this page are planning inputs, not approved specifications or universal performance claims.

How are MOQ, samples, and lead time confirmed?

MOQ, sample cost, sample rounds, and lead time are confirmed after reviewing quantity by one-piece or two-piece route, race format, sleeve, fit block, fabric and chamois code, colorway, athlete size, name or identity, artwork and sponsor version, packing group, destination country, and event date. No universal figure on this page is a quotation or production commitment.

What evidence should be checked before bulk release?

Bulk-release evidence: Approve an identified wet-and-dry project sample, garment measurements, swim, cycling-position, run and transition notes, coverage and opacity, zipper, chamois, grippers, pockets, seam comfort, color and artwork, athlete allocation, current-rule review, packing record, and written release. Branding handoff: Control club, team, athlete or country identity, sponsor hierarchy, front, back, side, sleeve and leg artwork, number zone, size-dependent scaling, event marks, labels, and athlete, team, event, or retail packing.
