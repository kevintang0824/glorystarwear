Range and silhouette planning

  • Weightlifting competition singlets, training singlets, warm-up jackets and pants, club T-shirts, coach apparel, socks, bags, and coordinated team merchandise.
  • Confirm torso girth, shoulder strap, neckline, armhole, back and side coverage, hip and seat balance, thigh opening, crotch length, men's, women's, youth, or custom athlete blocks, and allocated size.
  • Buyer and use context: weightlifting clubs, schools, universities, federations, events, sports brands, private labels, retailers, and teamwear distributors.

Material and construction review

  • State target composition, weight, stretch and recovery, opacity, handfeel, surface friction, color and decoration route on coded swatches; compression, durability, or performance language requires an agreed test.
  • Review shoulder straps, neckline and armhole finish, side and inseam route, crotch and seat shape, leg opening, low-profile labels, bar-contact or belt zones, and decoration placement.
  • Define deep squat positions, clean and jerk, snatch, overhead lockout, hip and shoulder mobility, bar contact, platform heat, belt or support use, warm-up, and repeated training conditions.

Branding, order, and approval control

  • Control club, federation or school identity, athlete name, country or team marks, sponsor positions, transfers, sublimation or embroidery, labels, and athlete, team, event, or retail packing.
  • Quantity by competition, training, warm-up, staff, or retail item, fit block, colorway, athlete size, name, club or country identity, sponsor version, spare allocation, packing group, destination, and event date.
  • Approve an identified singlet sample, measurements, squat, pull and overhead movement notes, opacity and recovery review, bar-contact observations, artwork, athlete roster, weigh-in or event instructions, pack plan, and reorder reference.

Sport-specific decision map

Separate weightlifting fit, bar-contact construction, weigh-in context, and athlete allocation

A weightlifting competition singlet is not a wrestling uniform or a compression underlayer. Confirm the current event rules, intended use, athlete block, movement, construction, artwork, and approval responsibility before bulk release.

01

Competition costume versus training suit

Name the governing body, event, age group, competition costume, training version, warm-up pieces, belt or support use, branding restrictions, and approval deadline. Check the current event TCRR and organizer instructions; this page and a supplier sample do not certify eligibility.

02

Deep squat and overhead fit

Review torso girth, strap position, neckline, armhole, side and back coverage, crotch length, seat, hip, thigh opening, and leg hem through the snatch, clean, jerk, deep squat, pull, and overhead lockout. Record restriction, gaping, riding, exposure, or seam pressure by athlete and size.

03

Bar, belt, seam, and decoration zones

Map bar-contact, belt, torso, hip, thigh, label, seam, transfer, patch, and logo positions. Review surface, bulk, edge hardness, backing, stretch, opacity, and decoration adhesion on the production-intent material without turning requested reinforcement into an untested claim.

04

Athlete samples, weigh-in, and team packs

Link athlete, fit block, garment size, sample identity, competition and training versions, club or country artwork, sponsors, spares, individual pack, carton, destination, and reorder code. Record the applicable weigh-in or costume procedure supplied by the organizer.
