Wet-use sample protocol

Release each garment against its own construction evidence

A rash guard and a board short can share artwork, but they do not share the same fit, seam, closure, pocket, drainage, wet-recovery, or safety questions. Approve two identified garments and record the dry and conditioned states used for every comparison.

Brief the sample

Send decisions that separate a surf set from generic swimwear

Rash guard brief

Include wearer and activity, fit route, body and sleeve lengths, neckline, panel map, stretch direction, seam preference, hem behavior, print zones, size range and dry or conditioned checks.

Board short brief

Include waist construction, fly or closure, drawcord, rise, inseam, leg opening, side slit, pocket and drainage design, liner, reinforcement, hardware, artwork and movement checks.

Paired-set control

Use one approved color and artwork system while keeping separate material codes, measurement charts, construction callouts, sample IDs, defect examples and release decisions.

Reorder evidence

Retain material lots, output files, physical references, measurements, test or wear results, accepted deviations and component versions; recheck any later substitution.
