Buyer inspection guide

Sportswear AQL and Pre-Shipment Inspection Checklist

Define the lot, sampling method, defect classifications, approved references, inspection evidence, and release decision before finished sportswear leaves production.

Direct answer

What is an AQL inspection for sportswear?

An AQL inspection uses an agreed sampling plan to decide whether a finished lot meets a buyer-defined acceptance rule. The plan should name the lot, unit, standard and edition, inspection level, sampling scheme, defect classifications, AQL values or alternative limits, approved references, and decision authority. AQL is not a promise that every garment is defect free.

Define the lot

Record the order, style, color, size range, finished quantity, packing completion, inspection location, and unit being counted.

Choose the sampling plan

Name the standard, edition, inspection level, sampling scheme, any switching rules, and the resulting sample and decision criteria.

Classify defects first

Agree critical, major, and minor definitions for the actual product, market, artwork, roster, labels, and packaging before inspection.

Retain the decision record

Keep sampled quantities, measurements, defect evidence, counts by class, corrective action, reinspection need, approver, and release status.

2026 standards note

Record the exact standard and edition

ISO 2859-1:2026 describes sampling procedures for inspection by attributes and replaced the earlier edition in 2026. This guide does not reproduce ISO sampling tables. Obtain the authorized standard, agree how it applies to the order, and record the selected plan in the inspection brief.

  • AQL is an index used with the selected sampling plan, not simply an allowed defect percentage.
  • The buyer and supplier should agree the inspection unit, lot formation, defect classes, AQL or other acceptance rule, and decision authority.
  • Regulatory, safety, contractual, or brand-critical requirements may need zero-tolerance rules, testing, or other controls outside an ordinary visual sampling plan.
  • Confirm current market and product requirements with qualified compliance or inspection professionals where needed.

Seven-step workflow

Plan the inspection before the inspector opens a carton

Late decisions about defect severity or approved references make a pass or fail result difficult to defend.

  1. Confirm readiness and lot formation

    Define how much production and packing must be complete, which units form the lot, where it is stored, and whether reworked or split lots are identified separately.

  2. Release the inspection brief

    Name the standard and edition, inspection level, sampling scheme, AQL or alternative acceptance rule by defect class, inspection method, and decision authority.

  3. Lock the references

    Provide the released tech pack, approved sample, material codes, size chart, artwork, roster, label files, packaging reference, order, and packing list.

  4. Select units across the lot

    Record how cartons, styles, colors, sizes, and units were chosen so the sample is not limited to convenient or preselected pieces.

  5. Inspect and document consistently

    Use the same measurement method, lighting or comparison conditions, defect definitions, photo naming, unit identification, and evidence rule throughout.

  6. Apply the agreed decision plan

    Count and classify results according to the selected method. Do not change a defect class after seeing whether the lot would pass unless the authorized parties document a justified correction.

  7. Control corrective action and release

    State whether the lot is accepted, held, reworked, sorted, replaced, or reinspected, then identify the approver and retain the final report with shipment records.

Limits of sampling

AQL inspection complements production control

A pre-shipment sample can support a lot decision, but it cannot replace product development, process control, or evidence required for every unit.

Not sample approval

Use a separate sportswear sample approval checklist to close fit, material, artwork, labels, and packing before bulk release.

Not in-line control

Use the sportswear quality control checklist to connect incoming materials, first production, in-line defects, measurements, packing, and final release.

Not a complete specification

Use a controlled sportswear tech pack so the inspector is not forced to infer construction, tolerances, artwork, or packaging requirements.

Not a universal compliance decision

Review destination-market documents and testing separately. A sampled visual inspection does not establish every legal, chemical, performance, or labeling requirement.

FAQ

Sportswear AQL inspection questions

What does AQL mean in garment inspection?

AQL means acceptance quality limit. In an agreed sampling system, it is an index used with the lot size, inspection level, sampling scheme, and defect classification to determine a sample and acceptance decision.

Is AQL the same as an allowed defect percentage?

No. An AQL value is not simply the percentage of defects a buyer agrees to receive. It is used within a defined sampling plan, so the selected standard, edition, inspection level, sample size, defect classes, and acceptance rules must be recorded together.

Which AQL level should a sportswear buyer use?

There is no universal value for every garment or defect class. Buyers should select and document rules according to product risk, intended use, destination requirements, brand standard, contractual terms, supplier history, and qualified inspection advice.

Does passing pre-shipment inspection guarantee every garment is defect free?

No. A sampling inspection supports a decision about the inspected lot under the agreed plan. It does not inspect every unit or replace approved samples, specifications, process control, testing, compliance review, or corrective action.
