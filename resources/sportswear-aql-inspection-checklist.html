Direct answer

What is an AQL inspection for sportswear?

An AQL inspection applies an agreed sampling plan and acceptance rule to a defined production lot. The inspection does not prove that every unit is defect-free. It helps the buyer make a consistent accept, reject, hold, or corrective-action decision from a documented sample, defect classification, and product specification.

2026 standard note

Use the current sampling standard without copying a generic table

ISO 2859-1:2026 covers sampling procedures for inspection by attributes and replaced the 1999 edition. The buyer, supplier, and inspector should identify the standard edition, lot size, inspection level, sample size code, acceptance quality limit, and switching or other applicable rules in the approved inspection instruction.

  • Do not choose a sample size from an unrelated online table without confirming the standard, edition, lot definition, and plan.
  • Do not treat an AQL value as a promise that the same percentage of the shipment is defective.
  • Do not let statistical sampling replace safety checks, legal requirements, approved specifications, quantity verification, or shipment-specific risk controls.

Inspection setup

Prepare the brief before booking the inspector

The report can only be as clear as the inspection instruction. Close the commercial and technical inputs before the visit.

  1. Identify the shipment lot

    Record purchase order, supplier, factory, location, style, color, size range, order quantity, packed quantity, carton count, inspection date, and required shipment date.

  2. Confirm readiness

    State the required production and packing completion, whether goods must be fully presented, and what happens when the lot is incomplete or inaccessible.

  3. Release one specification set

    Provide current tech packs, measurement charts, approved samples, fabric and color references, artwork, label files, packaging files, rosters, and order breakdowns.

  4. Write the sampling instruction

    Identify the agreed standard and edition, lot definition, inspection level, sampling plan, AQL or other acceptance rule, and any separate rules for safety or high-risk checks.

  5. Define defects with examples

    Map product-specific defects to critical, major, or minor classes. Add photos, locations, measurement rules, and market requirements where words could be interpreted differently.

  6. List tests and quantities

    State which units are checked for measurements, appearance, function, labels, packing, color, artwork, roster data, barcode, carton marks, or other agreed tests.

  7. Set the release workflow

    Name the report recipient, decision owner, exception authority, corrective-action evidence, reinspection rule, shipment hold, and document retention requirement.

FAQ

Sportswear AQL inspection questions

Does passing an AQL inspection mean every garment is defect-free?

No. A sampling inspection evaluates selected units from a defined lot under an agreed plan. Passing supports the specified lot decision, but it does not prove that every uninspected unit has no defect.

Who should choose the AQL and inspection level?

The buyer should agree the sampling and acceptance rules with the supplier and inspector using product risk, market requirements, contract terms, lot structure, and the applicable standard. A generic value copied from another order may not fit the project.

Can one inspection cover every style and color in an order?

Only if the approved lot definition and sampling plan support that treatment. Different factories, products, colors, size groups, production conditions, or risk profiles may need separate lots, stratified selection, or separate decisions.

What happens when a sportswear inspection fails?

Follow the written hold and escalation plan. Identify affected lots and defects, review root cause and corrective action, define sorting or repair evidence, decide whether reinspection is required, and obtain written release before shipment.
