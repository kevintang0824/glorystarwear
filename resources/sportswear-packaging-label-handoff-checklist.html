Direct answer

What should a sportswear packaging specification include?

A controlled sportswear packaging specification should identify the purchase order, style, colorway, size, SKU, sales channel, and destination; define every garment label, hangtag, sticker, barcode, individual pack, inner pack, carton, and shipping mark; state artwork, text, material, dimensions, placement, folding, pack quantity, assortment, and carton sequence; and link the physical packed sample, barcode scan test, approval, inspection evidence, and shipment-release decision. Market, retailer, marketplace, warehouse, and carrier requirements should be confirmed for the actual destination instead of copied from a generic template.

SKU and barcode control

Keep product identity separate from the printed symbol

A SKU, GTIN or other identifier, barcode symbol, artwork file, and physical printed label are connected but not interchangeable. The party responsible for product identity should supply or approve the data; the supplier should not invent commercial identifiers from visual artwork.

Build order

Create the handoff before bulk packing starts

Packaging decisions affect components, artwork, labeling, SKU data, physical pack size, carton counts, inspection, shipping volume, and warehouse receipt. Resolve them early enough to sample the complete packed product.

  1. Freeze the sellable-unit map

    List every active style-color-size or personalized variant, sales channel, destination, SKU, identifier, pack type, and responsible data owner.

  2. Define each packaging component

    Assign a component ID and record its layer, type, material, construction, dimensions, color, artwork, text, attachment, and supplier-confirmed specification.

  3. Connect labels to the right unit

    Map garment labels, hangtags, stickers, barcodes, inserts, bags, and boxes to the exact SKU or controlled multi-SKU scope.

  4. Build the fold and pack hierarchy

    Define garment orientation, fold method, individual units, inner or bundle pack, master carton quantity, assortment, sequence, and destination.

  5. Approve the physical packed sample

    Use the actual garment and components to check fit, presentation, protection, labels, barcode scan, opening, repacking, and visible identifiers.

  6. Reconcile before shipment release

    Compare purchase-order totals, SKU quantities, packed units, carton counts, carton labels, inspection evidence, and released packing list; approve every exception in writing.

Data

FAQ

Sportswear packaging and label questions

Who should provide the SKU and barcode data?

The buyer, brand, retailer, marketplace, or other party responsible for product identity should provide or approve the data and mapping rules. The supplier can print and apply approved symbols, but should not invent commercial identifiers from a product photo or packaging mockup.

Does every color and size need a separate packaging row?

Create a separate row whenever the SKU, identifier, label, barcode, size sticker, artwork, text, pack component, fold, quantity, assortment, destination, or approval evidence changes. One row can cover multiple variants only when every controlled condition is identical and the scope is explicit.

Are care labels the same for every market?

No. Label content and placement can depend on the product, fiber, origin, responsible business, care basis, target market, language, sales channel, and current rules. Assign a qualified owner to confirm the actual destination requirements.

When should packaging be approved?

Approve artwork and component specifications before production, then approve a physical packed sample using the actual garment, labels, packaging, barcode, fold, and pack method before bulk packing. Recheck carton data and shipment totals during final inspection.

Can packaging change after approval?

Only through a documented change review. Identify the affected SKU, component, artwork, text, barcode, pack quantity, carton plan, cost, timing, inventory, sample, inspection, and destination requirement before releasing the revision.

Need a packaging route?

Send the product map, packaging references, SKU data, and destination requirements.

Unknown component details can remain open, but commercial identifiers, content ownership, approval responsibility, and release conditions should be explicit.
